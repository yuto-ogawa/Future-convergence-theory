# Figshare Account Termination Record

**Date:** 2026-07-13

## Purpose

This document records the factual sequence regarding the Figshare account associated with the FCT papers.

Its purpose is to preserve the continuity of the publication history and to document the circumstances surrounding the associated DOI records.

---

## Timeline

### Initial State

- FCT papers were published through Figshare.
- DOIs were assigned to the publications.

### Account Action

- The associated Figshare account became inaccessible.
- An inquiry was submitted requesting clarification regarding the account status and the associated research records.

### Discussion

The correspondence raised the following structural questions:

1. Why was the content accepted and assigned a DOI before the subsequent account termination?
2. How does post-publication account termination relate to the persistence expected from DOI-assigned scholarly records?
3. How is this process consistent with the role of a long-term scholarly repository?

### Final Response

Figshare replied that:

- the suspension decision is final;
- no appeal process exists; and
- metadata and files can be provided upon request.

No substantive explanation was provided regarding the repository-level design questions raised above.

---

## Current Status (Observed)

As of 2026-07-13,

- The DOI remains assigned.
- The associated Figshare account has been terminated.
- The DOI no longer provides access to the original publication in its published form.
- The original publication can no longer be accessed through its original Figshare publication route.

---

## Consequence for FCT

Based on the above observations, FCT no longer regards DOI assignment alone as sufficient to guarantee the long-term permanence of its reference chain.

Future work will therefore investigate publication architectures that do not rely solely on a single publication platform or identifier system.

---

## Formal Questions Submitted to Figshare

The following issues were formally raised to Figshare during the correspondence regarding the termination of the author's account. These questions constitute the basis of the discussion and document the structural concerns motivating the inquiry.

### 1. Basis of the Decision

- Which specific content, manuscript, action, or provision of the Terms and Conditions was determined to have been violated?
- Was the account disabled through an automated process or manual review?
- What factual basis supported the termination decision?

### 2. Review Process

- Whether the decision was final.
- Whether any review or appeal mechanism existed.
- If no appeal mechanism existed, how the correctness of either the original acceptance decision or the later suspension decision could ever be examined.

### 3. Status of the Research Record

- Whether the associated publication record had been permanently deleted or merely rendered inaccessible.
- Whether restoration of the publication record and the associated account remained possible.
- Whether the removal should be regarded as reversible or irreversible.

### 4. Preservation of Research Origin

The correspondence explained that, from the author's perspective, the published record constituted the original public record of the research.

Accordingly, permanent removal without restoration would constitute the irreversible loss of that research origin on the Figshare platform.

### 5. DOI Stewardship

The correspondence questioned the consistency between:

- assigning persistent DOIs to research outputs;
- accepting those outputs into the scholarly record;
- subsequently terminating the author's account permanently;
- providing no appeal mechanism; and
- continuing to present DOI registration as a reliable mechanism for long-term scholarly persistence.

### 6. Publication Sequence

The correspondence argued that the logical publication sequence should be:

1. Submission
2. Policy review
3. DOI assignment
4. Public publication

rather than:

1. Submission
2. DOI assignment
3. Public publication
4. Permanent account termination without appeal

The concern raised was that DOI assignment acknowledges a publication as part of the scholarly record before the platform later permanently excludes the author from maintaining that same record.

### 7. DOI Persistence

The correspondence further argued that DOI persistence is not limited to the continued existence of an identifier.

Rather, persistence also requires continued access to the scholarly record represented by that identifier and the continued ability for that scholarly record to remain meaningfully maintained within the publication infrastructure.

### 8. Metadata and File Export

Although Figshare offered to provide metadata and files, the correspondence explained that exported files are not equivalent to maintaining an authoritative scholarly record.

The concern raised was that file export alone does not resolve the structural role of a DOI as a persistent scholarly reference.

### 9. Structural Question

The correspondence concluded by asking Figshare to explain how the above conditions are considered compatible with its role as a DOI registration and scholarly publication platform.

The purpose of the correspondence was therefore not merely to dispute an individual moderation decision, but to request an explanation of the institutional design governing DOI stewardship and the long-term preservation of scholarly records.
