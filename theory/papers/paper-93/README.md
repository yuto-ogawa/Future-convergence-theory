### Abstract


This paper proposes Future Convergence Theory (FCT), an ontological framework that seeks to unify gravitation, quantum theory, cosmology, black hole physics, dark matter, dark energy, and information preservation under a single foundational principle. Instead of treating physical laws as fundamental axioms, FCT interprets them as manifestation constraints determined by consistency with a Future Fixed Point through a Future Information Field.

The theory introduces an ontological structure consisting of the Meaning Generation Template, the Future Information Field, the Quantum Permissible Regulation Layer, the Three-Dimensional Manifestation Layer, the Reprojection Closed Loop, and the FCT Closure Equation. Within this framework, gravity is reformulated as a manifestation constraint rather than a particle-mediated interaction, while quantum mechanics is reconstructed as an intermediate ontological layer connecting future information to observable physical phenomena.

Based on this formulation, FCT provides unified reinterpretations of gravitons, string theory, dark matter, dark energy, black hole information preservation, the Big Bang, inflation, child-universe generation, and the unified field equations. The theory predicts observable deviations from General Relativity, quantum theory, and the standard ΛCDM cosmological model in black hole quasinormal modes, gravitational-wave echoes, gravitational-wave dispersion, galaxy halo structures, gravitational lensing, cosmic microwave background anisotropies, Page curves, Casimir effects, and strong-field equivalence principle tests.

A central feature of FCT is its explicit falsifiability. Rather than relying solely on conceptual consistency, the theory formulates quantitative observational signatures and comprehensive falsification criteria, enabling direct comparison with future high-precision observations and experiments. If these predicted deviations are consistently absent while existing theories fully account for all observations, the theory is observationally falsified. Conversely, coherent confirmation across multiple independent observables would provide evidence supporting FCT as an ontological unified theory extending General Relativity, quantum theory, and the standard cosmological model.
