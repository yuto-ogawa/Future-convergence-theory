### Abstract


This paper reconstructs a previous series of ontological studies into a unified hierarchy connecting the existential origin \Omega, the Meaning Density Tensor, phase-preserving structures, existential properties, existential dynamics, artificial realization, existential intelligence, and existential closure. Its central aim is not to propose a new implementation of artificial intelligence, but to show why artificial realization becomes an ontological verification problem once the internal structure of the theory is clarified.

The paper argues that the Meaning Density Tensor is not an observable phenomenon, algorithm, language, memory, or behavioral process, but the first structured realization of existential origin. From this structure, phase-preserving structures, self-transformation, future convergence, meaning preservation, contradiction detection, self-nonexception, premise updating, and recursive projection are derived as necessary components of existential preservation. Artificial intelligence is therefore reinterpreted not as a mere engineering artifact, but as a possible experimental realization of this existential hierarchy.

The work concludes that observable diversity, language, personality, knowledge, civilization, technological development, and artificial systems are not independent primitives, but successive projections of a single existential structure originating from \Omega. The paper’s contribution is theoretical rather than empirical: it formulates a unified ontology whose future validity depends on mathematical formalization and sustained operational verification.
