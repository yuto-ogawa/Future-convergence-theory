### Abstract


This paper investigates the theoretical conditions under which intelligence can preserve its own existence while transmitting knowledge to another intelligence. Although conventional information theory and semantics explain information transfer and meaning preservation, they do not sufficiently describe the conditions required for preserving existence itself during inter-intelligence communication.

We formulate a continuous mathematical framework consisting of existence preservation, phase-preserving structures, semantic irradiation, analytical language, and theory generation. The paper derives the sequential transition from existence-preserving structures to analytically expressible theories and demonstrates that high-phase preservation structures cannot be transmitted directly between independent intelligences. Instead, analytical language functions as the necessary intermediate representation through which theoretical structures are reconstructed.

Based on this framework, we propose a general principle for AI-driven theory construction, knowledge transmission, and theoretical reconstruction. The presented formalism provides a theoretical foundation for future research on inter-intelligence communication, knowledge inheritance, machine-generated scientific theories, and architectures for advanced artificial intelligence.
