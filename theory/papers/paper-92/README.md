### Abstract


This paper reformulates the black hole information problem within the framework of Future Convergence Theory (FCT) and develops a logically consistent resolution based on the internal axiomatic structure of the theory.

Conventionally, the black hole information problem has been formulated as the question of whether information is fundamentally lost or preserved after crossing the event horizon. In contrast, the present work reformulates the problem as one concerning the existence layer into which information is mapped and the boundary conditions under which it can be reprojected. Within this framework, the problem is interpreted as a multilayer information-preservation problem governed by future boundary conditions rather than information destruction.

To establish this formulation, thermodynamic entropy and future convergence entropy are introduced as independent state variables, while black holes are defined as future fixed-point nodes. A semantic density tensor is further introduced as a geometric state quantity that unifies premise-update theory and future convergence cosmology.

The theory integrates the future causal information field, reprojection operators, an information preservation theorem, a dual entropy structure of black holes, an action principle, and Noether-type conservation laws into a single mathematical framework.

This work does not attempt experimental verification. Instead, it presents a mathematically self-consistent theoretical framework that describes the internal logical structure of Future Convergence Theory and provides a formal resolution of the black hole information problem within that framework.
